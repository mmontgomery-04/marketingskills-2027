# Customer Advocacy

## Before You Start

1. **Load shared context.** If `.agents/marketing-context.md` exists, read it first and only ask for what's missing. If it doesn't exist, offer to run the [marketing-context](../marketing-context/SKILL.md) skill (5–10 minutes) or proceed with a few quick questions.
2. **Confirm the job.** Restate the task, the audience, and what "done" looks like in one or two sentences before producing work.
3. **Write like a person.** Any copy you produce (emails, posts, ads, pages, briefs) follows the [copywriting's Don'ts](../copywriting/SKILL.md): no em dashes, no "it's not X, it's Y" framing, no choppy fragment stacks or repetition for effect; use flowing prose in second person with contractions.

Buyers trust other customers far more than they trust vendors, and AI assistants frequently cite review sites and customer stories when recommending products. Advocacy turns happy customers into proof that works across sales, website conversion, paid ads, and AI search.

## Workflow

### 1. Find advocates
Look for customers with strong outcomes and goodwill: high NPS or CSAT responses, strong product usage, renewals and expansions, positive support interactions, and champions who've been promoted. Ask customer success and sales for nominations every month.

### 2. Make the ask at the right moment
Ask soon after a win: a successful launch, a renewal, a milestone, a great support experience, or a strong survey score. Make it easy, and offer a range of asks from small (a review, a quote) to large (a case study, a speaking slot).

### 3. Produce the proof
- Case studies: [case-studies.md](references/case-studies.md)
- Reviews: [reviews-program.md](references/reviews-program.md)
- References, advisory boards, and co-marketing: [advocacy-program.md](references/advocacy-program.md)

### 4. Distribute everywhere proof matters
Homepage and pricing page, comparison pages, sales decks and follow-ups, ads (quotes and metrics as creative), email nurture, social posts (customer stories and tagged quotes), and event speaking slots.

### 5. Recognize and retain advocates
Thank advocates personally, give them visibility (spotlights, speaking roles, awards), early access to features, and community status. Track every ask so no customer gets asked too often.

## Rules
- Get written approval for every name, logo, quote, and metric before publishing, and follow the customer's PR and legal requirements.
- Never write fake reviews or testimonials, and never pay for positive reviews. Review incentives must be allowed by the platform's rules, offered for any honest review regardless of sentiment, and disclosed where required. The FTC's rule on fake reviews and testimonials applies in the US.
- Quotes are the customer's words. You can tighten them for length with approval, but never invent claims.
