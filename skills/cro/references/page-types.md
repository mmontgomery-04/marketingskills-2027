# CRO by Page Type

What to check first on each kind of page, and tests worth running there. Use it alongside [page-audit.md](page-audit.md).

## Homepage
Visitors arrive at every stage, from first-time browsers to buyers ready to talk.
- Name the category and the audience on the first screen.
- Offer two paths: a primary CTA for ready buyers and a lighter one (product tour, customer stories, pricing) for researchers.
- Route by role, industry, or use case ("For finance teams →") so nobody has to read everything.
- **Tests:** outcome headline vs category headline; demo CTA vs interactive tour; proof bar directly under the hero vs lower down.

## Paid or campaign landing page
- One audience, one offer, one CTA. Remove the global nav and other exits.
- Repeat the ad's promise in the headline, in close to the same words.
- Make the whole case on the page; ad visitors rarely go browsing for more.
- **Tests:** offer type (demo vs guide vs calculator); form on the page vs form after a click; short page vs long page for colder audiences.

## Pricing page
Most visitors here are working out which plan fits, or whether they need to talk to sales.
- Mark a recommended plan and say in one line who each plan is for.
- Show plan contents in a comparison table and explain limits (seats, usage, overages) in plain words.
- Answer billing, contract, and security questions in an FAQ beside the plans.
- Give enterprise buyers a clear "Talk to sales" route without hiding prices from everyone else.
- **Tests:** annual vs monthly as the default; plan order; a short "which plan fits?" helper; showing a mid-tier price vs "contact us".

## Product or feature page
- Open with the job the feature does and the result, then show it (screenshot, short video, clickable demo).
- Include one use case or customer example.
- Offer next steps that match interest: try it, see it in a demo, read the docs.
- **Tests:** video vs static screenshots; trial CTA vs demo CTA.

## Demo request page
- Say what the demo covers, how long it takes, and who they'll meet.
- Keep the form short and qualify with enrichment ([form-optimization.md](form-optimization.md)).
- Put proof and a short objection FAQ beside the form.
- **Tests:** instant scheduler after submit vs "we'll be in touch"; fewer fields; a "what you'll see" list above the form.

## Blog and resource pages
Readers came for the content, so the offer has to fit the topic. A post on lead scoring should offer a lead scoring template, not a generic demo.
- Put CTAs where a reader naturally pauses: after the opening answer, after a key section, and at the end.
- **Tests:** an in-line content upgrade vs an end-of-post CTA; sidebar vs in-body placement.
