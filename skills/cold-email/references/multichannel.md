# Multichannel & LinkedIn

Email is usually the main channel, but prospects who also see your name on LinkedIn, hear a voicemail, or notice an ad reply more often. The day-by-day default cadence lives in [prospecting](../../prospecting/SKILL.md); this file covers the rules around it.

## Ground rules
- **One person, one active sequence.** Never run two sequences at the same contact.
- **A reply anywhere stops everything.** Email, LinkedIn, phone, or a form fill: all automated steps for that person end, and often for their colleagues at the account too.
- **Wire the tools together.** Most sending tools can fire an event on reply, bounce, or unsubscribe. Use those events (or CRM workflows) to stop other channels and log the outcome.

## Channels by account tier
| Tier | Channels |
|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Email, LinkedIn, phone, a short personal video, account ads, occasional direct mail |
| Tier 2 | Email, LinkedIn, phone |
| Tier 3 | Email, light LinkedIn |

## LinkedIn
- **Profile first.** A real photo and a headline that says who you help ("Helping finance teams close faster"), not just a job title. Prospects check it before replying.
- **Warm up before connecting.** View their profile, leave a thoughtful comment on a recent post, then send the request.
- **Connection request:** blank or one plain line. No pitch.
- **First message after they accept:** build on something real (their post, a shared group, the email you sent). Keep it short and end with a question.
- **Limits:** LinkedIn caps weekly invitations and restricts accounts that look spammy. Check current limits and stay well under them.
- **Automation:** tools that operate your account for you break LinkedIn's User Agreement and can get the account restricted. If the user chooses to use them anyway, say so plainly and keep volumes low.

## Phone and video
- Call Tier 1 and 2 contacts after the first or second email, so your name is familiar. Leave a short voicemail that points to the email.
- A 60-second personal video (their site or LinkedIn on screen) works for Tier 1, not for volume.
