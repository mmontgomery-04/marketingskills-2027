# On-Page SEO Checklist (Blogs)

Source: William Montgomery's On-Page SEO Checklist.

## Title tag
- 60–70 characters, primary keyword near the start.
- Model it after the top 3–4 ranking titles. "[Keyword]: Process, Examples…"-style titles still work.

## H1
- Can run a bit longer than the title and slip in a secondary keyword or variant.
- Example: **Title:** How to Enable Telemetry for D365 Finance Security Role Optimization. **H1:** Enabling Telemetry in Microsoft Dynamics 365 to Optimize Security Roles & Licensing.

## Meta description
- 155–160 characters.
- Clearly define or explain the query/broader topic, or tell readers what they'll learn ("Learn how to…", "Find out…").

## Intro
- Use the primary keyword early.
- Answer the broader query directly without overexplaining; save details for the H2s.
- Tell readers what they'll learn.

## Table of contents
- Add one when the article is longer than ~1,000 words.

## Headers
- Mirror top-ranking formats while keeping an original structure.
- The outline should make narrative sense and serve humans, engines, and LLMs, with question-based H2s.

## Formatting
- Bulleted lists in the right sections and clear semantic cues for engines and LLMs.

## Content length
- Depth over word count. Pillar pages ~2.5K–3K words, sub-blogs ~1K–1.5K. Use as many words as you need and don't fill space.

## Keyword coverage
- Primary keyword in key headers; take phrasing inspiration from competitors.
- Sprinkle related terms across subheaders and body copy, leaning on question-based keyword research.

## Image naming
- Primary keyword in the featured image's file name and alt text, hyphenated (e.g. `d365-user-security-governance`).
- Match body images to H2s that use related keywords and use those keywords in their file names and alt text.

## Linking
- **Internal:** a few links to relevant pages to prove topical authority.
- **Outbound:** always cite external sources for stats and third-party data.

## FAQs
- Add 3–5 based on People Also Ask to capture long-tail and featured-snippet potential.
- Fill gaps with a prompt such as: "Come up with 3–5 FAQs for this article based on questions ICPs are likely to search related to this topic or readers are likely to ask after reading the article in full: [article]".

## Voice note
Keep the brand's voice and tone, but be clear, direct, and almost "simple" when answering key H2 questions and FAQs. No fluff, buzzwords, or bombastic copy.
