# Influencer Marketing (B2B)

## Before You Start

1. **Load shared context.** If `.agents/marketing-context.md` exists, read it first and only ask for what's missing. If it doesn't exist, offer to run the [marketing-context](../marketing-context/SKILL.md) skill (5–10 minutes) or proceed with a few quick questions.
2. **Confirm the job.** Restate the task, the audience, and what "done" looks like in one or two sentences before producing work.

## B2B is different
B2B influence comes from **trusted practitioners with niche audiences**: a RevOps leader with 25K engaged LinkedIn followers beats a celebrity with 2M. Buyers trust peers, not ads.

## Workflow

### 1. Goal
Awareness in a new segment, pipeline for a campaign, credibility for a launch, or content you can repurpose? The goal decides the model.

### 2. Find creators
Search where your ICP learns: LinkedIn (search by topic, check commenters on industry posts), niche newsletters, YouTube, podcasts, Slack/Discord communities, conference speakers. Also look at your own customers and employees.

### 3. Vet
| Check | Look for |
|---|---|
| Audience fit | Commenters are your ICP (titles, companies) |
| Engagement quality | Real comments, not pods or generic emoji |
| Credibility | Actual practitioner experience |
| Brand safety | Past posts, controversies, competitor deals |
| Consistency | Posts regularly, stable growth |

### 4. Choose a model
See [partnership-models.md](references/partnership-models.md).

### 5. Brief and contract
Use [briefs-and-contracts.md](references/briefs-and-contracts.md). Give creators the message and the facts, not a script. Their voice is the point.

### 6. Measure
Unique links/UTMs and codes, self-reported attribution ("How did you hear about us?"), branded search lift, follower and engagement growth, and pipeline influenced. Expect dark-social effects that tracking undercounts.
